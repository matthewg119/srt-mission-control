## Two questions to answer first, because they are questions and not defects

Matthew asked: *"is step 13 for creating posts for my clients? or what is it exactly and what
is the resemblance between step 12?"*

> **Step 12 and step 13 come from the same corpus and do opposite jobs.**
>
> **Step 12, `custom_question_set`**, is the MEASUREMENT set: 40 or 60 questions, approved on
> the call, frozen at Day 0. It is what the day 30, 60 and 90 numbers are scored against.
> Nothing is ever published from it.
>
> **Step 13, `page_candidates`**, is the PUBLISHING backlog: the same phrases scored and
> ranked for which ones are worth writing a page about, with `currently_named` as a tri-state
> so a question the engines already name them for can be skipped.
>
> One says what we measure. The other says what we write. Both cards must say so, the way
> steps 8, 16 and 30 now say what makes each of them different.

And: *"I want to know how we can do it ourselves so claude doesnt label our text content with
the watermark or whatever that google started reading as spam."*

> **There is no watermark in Claude's text output and Google does not read one.** SynthID is
> an image, audio and video technology; there is no text watermark on Anthropic API output.
> What Google's policy actually penalises is scaled, unhelpful, unedited content at volume,
> which is a fact about the writing rather than about who typed it.
>
> So **no third-party API is needed and none is being added.** Moving publishing to a third
> party would also be worse in a way that matters here: the pages exist so that an engine
> answering a buyer's question has something **on the client's own domain** to cite.
> `learn.{clientdomain}` already publishes, is indexed, has a per-host `sitemap.xml` and an
> `llms.txt`, carries `QAPage` markup, and the Day 0 wall already guards it.
>
> What this lane DOES give him is the thing that actually answers the concern: **a lane where
> his own words go in verbatim** and a model only touches them if he explicitly asks.

## 4B. The editor on the board

Matthew: *"if we want to make any edits, we need to make sure we can edit it like a word
document or you know at least just select the font style, size and other stuff."*

- The Hub panel (`src/components/clients/hub-form.tsx`) gains a real editing surface for
  `answer_md`: a markdown toolbar (bold, italic, H2, lists, quote, link), a live preview, and
  Approve / Publish.

> **Font style and size are a THEME decision, not a per-page one, and the panel must say so.**
>
> `client_pages.answer_md` is rendered by `react-markdown` **without `rehype-raw`**,
> deliberately: a body is typed into a form and served on the client's own domain under their
> name, so a paste carrying a `<script>` would be an XSS on their site. Per-page inline
> styling means turning that off, on the one surface in this app that a client's customers
> visit.
>
> `theme.fontFamily` already exists, is validated by `safeFontFamily`, applies site-wide, and
> is the correct control. Point the panel at the Theme panel rather than shipping HTML
> injection, and say in one line why.

- **The Day 0 wall is untouched.** `page_publish` still refuses while
  `clients.day_0_archived_at` is null, and the check still goes **BEFORE** `setPublished`.
  That ordering is the point: publishing is not one write, it flips `client_pages.status`,
  ticks a delivery step, refreshes the checklist, posts a thread reply and **inserts a
  `client_messages` row telling the client their page is live**. A gate after any of that has
  already told the client something that should not have happened.
  Verify your work with `grep -rn "setPublished" src/` - `GATED` and `NOT_GATED` in
  `day-zero.ts` list every write path and why, and that grep is the hole check.
- Unpublishing is never gated. Taking a page down is the remedy, not the harm.
